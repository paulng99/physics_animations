Random Nature of Radioactive Decay

It cannot be predicted when a particular unstable nucleus will decay, but the probability of decay in a time interval can be estimated.

Key idea

  • • Every undecayed nucleus has the same probability of decay.
  • • We cannot know which nucleus will decay next.
  • • Temperature, pressure and chemical state do not change the decay rate.

What to conclude

  • The readings have no simple trend, even when the source is unchanged.
  • A single count represents one unstable nucleus decaying.
  • Many repeated readings reveal an average count rate.
  • This randomness is evidence that decay is probabilistic.
