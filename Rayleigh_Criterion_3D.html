Physics note

A circular aperture diffracts each point source into an Airy disk. Larger $d$ or shorter $\lambda$ shrinks $\theta_{\min}$, so two close objects become distinct. This is why a TEM (very small de Broglie $\lambda$) out-resolves an optical microscope, and why a wider lens or telescope objective has higher resolving power.

Rayleigh criterion — HKDSE

Two images are just resolvable when the centre of one diffraction pattern lies on the first minimum of the other.

For a circular aperture, lens or mirror the first dark ring is at $\theta \approx 1.22\,\lambda/d$ (HKDSE Data Booklet, Atomic World & Medical Physics).

A single slit uses $\lambda/a$ instead of $1.22\,\lambda/d$. The extra factor $1.22$ comes from the first zero of the Bessel function $J_1$ for circular diffraction.

Atomic World: a TEM uses electrons with de Broglie wavelength far shorter than visible light, so $\theta_{\min}$ is much smaller and resolving power is much higher.

Medical Physics: the resolving power of a lens is this diffraction-limited $\theta$ (or the corresponding object spacing $s \approx \theta L$).
