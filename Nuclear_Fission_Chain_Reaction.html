Physics notes (for teachers & students)

A thermal neutron can be captured by \(^{235}\mathrm{U}\), splitting it into two fission fragments and releasing typically 2–3 neutrons plus about 200 MeV, mainly as kinetic energy of the fragments (mass defect → \(E=mc^{2}\)).

A moderator (e.g. water or graphite) slows fast fission neutrons so they are more likely to cause further fissions. Control rods absorb neutrons and keep the multiplication factor \(k\) near 1 in a power plant.

This model is highly simplified: nuclei and neutrons are not to scale; escape, absorption and fission probabilities are tuned for classroom visibility; “bomb schematic” only shows abstract supercritical growth — no blast or casualty imagery.
