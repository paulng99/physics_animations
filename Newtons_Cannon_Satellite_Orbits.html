Physics note

Newton imagined a cannon on a tall mountain. At low $v$ the ball hits the ground nearby; raise $v$ and the landing point moves farther around the globe. When $v = \sqrt{GM/r}$, the path is a closed circle — a satellite. Between circular and escape speeds the orbit is an ellipse; above $v_{esc}=\sqrt{2GM/r}$ the ball never returns. Geostationary satellites sit much higher ($h\approx 36\,000\,\mathrm{km}$) so their period matches one sidereal day.

Circular motion & gravitation — HKDSE

Newton's law of universal gravitation: every mass attracts every other with $F = GMm/r^2$, directed along the line joining their centres.

For a circular orbit, gravity supplies the centripetal force: $GMm/r^2 = mv^2/r$, so $v_c=\sqrt{GM/r}$ and $T=2\pi\sqrt{r^3/GM}$.

Energy decides the orbit shape. With specific energy $\varepsilon=v^2/2-GM/r$: bound orbits ($\varepsilon<0$) are ellipses (circle when $e=0$); $\varepsilon=0$ is parabolic escape; $\varepsilon>0$ is hyperbolic.
