HKDSE relation

$$ L = 4\pi R^{2}\sigma T^{4} $$
$L$: luminosity (power radiated)
$R$: stellar radius
$T$: surface (effective) temperature
$\sigma$: Stefan–Boltzmann constant

A cooler but much larger red giant can outshine a hotter main-sequence star because luminosity grows with $R^{2}$ and $T^{4}$.

Physics note

Most stars lie on the main sequence, where hydrogen fuses to helium in the core. Higher-mass stars are hotter and more luminous, so they exhaust fuel sooner. After the main sequence a star expands into a red giant. Stars like the Sun end as white dwarfs; stars above about 8 solar masses explode as supernovae, leaving a neutron star or black hole.
