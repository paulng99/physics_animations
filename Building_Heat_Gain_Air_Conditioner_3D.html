Building Heat Gain, U-value & Air-Conditioner COP

Heat leaks into a cooled room through its envelope at a rate $Q/t = U A\,\Delta T$, and sunlight adds more through the window. An air conditioner must pump all of it back out, using electrical power $P = (Q/t)/\text{COP}$. Change the wall, window, shading and air conditioner and watch the energy bill respond.

Physics note

A thin layer of foam ($k$ = 0.035) adds far more thermal resistance $d/k$ than a thick concrete wall, so the $U$-value — and the conduction gain — collapses. Solar gain through glass does not depend on $\Delta T$ at all: only shading, tinting or a smaller window reduces it. The air conditioner is a heat pump; by energy conservation it dumps $Q + W$ outdoors, and a higher COP means less electrical work $W$ for the same cooling. Hong Kong's Building (Energy Efficiency) Regulation limits the OTTV of commercial building envelopes.

Building energy & COP — HKDSE E3

Thermal transmittance $U$ is the rate of heat flow through 1 m² of a building element for a 1 K temperature difference. For a single homogeneous layer $U = k/d$; the rate of heat transfer through area $A$ is $Q/t = U A\,\Delta T$.

Composite walls: resistances add in series, $1/U = d_1/k_1 + d_2/k_2 + \dots$, which is why a few centimetres of insulation dominate.

OTTV (overall thermal transfer value) averages the total heat gain through the envelope — conduction through opaque walls, conduction through glass and solar radiation through glass ($SC \cdot I \cdot A_{\text{win}}$) — over the envelope area. Lower OTTV means less cooling load.

Coefficient of performance of an air conditioner is $\text{COP} = Q_{\text{removed}}/W$, where $W$ is the electrical energy used. Since it moves heat rather than creating it, COP is normally 2.5–5. By energy conservation the outdoor unit rejects $Q_{\text{removed}} + W$.

Energy label: Hong Kong's Mandatory Energy Efficiency Labelling Scheme grades room coolers 1 (most efficient) to 5, based on their cooling seasonal performance factor.

Colour code: orange = heat conducted through the envelope and rejected outdoors, amber = solar radiation, sky blue = cooled air, green = electrical energy input.
